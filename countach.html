Lamborghini Countach

The Lamborghini Countach is one of Lamborghini’s most iconic supercars, produced from 1974 to 1990. It replaced the Miura and introduced a revolutionary wedge-shaped design created by Marcello Gandini, featuring sharp angles, dramatic proportions, and Lamborghini’s famous scissor doors. The Countach was powered by a naturally aspirated V12 engine, beginning with a 4.0-litre engine and later increasing to 5.2 litres in the final versions. Depending on the model, power output reached around 455 horsepower, with top speeds approaching 300 km/h.

During its production, Lamborghini released several versions of the Countach, including the LP400, LP400S, LP500S, 5000 QV, and 25th Anniversary. The later models introduced wider bodywork, larger engines, improved performance, and more aerodynamic features. Beyond its performance, the Countach became a symbol of the 1970s and 1980s supercar era and had a major influence on the design of future Lamborghini models. Its dramatic appearance and V12 performance helped establish the visual identity that Lamborghini remains known for today.
