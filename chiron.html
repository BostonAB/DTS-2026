Bugatti Chiron

The Bugatti Chiron was introduced in 2016 as the successor to the Bugatti Veyron. Powered by an 8.0-litre quad-turbocharged W16 engine producing 1,500 horsepower in its original version, it could reach a top speed of 420 km/h in its standard specification, with higher speeds achieved by specialised variants. The Chiron combined extreme performance with luxurious interiors, advanced engineering, and Bugatti’s distinctive design.

The Chiron became one of the most powerful and exclusive production hypercars in the world, with only 500 planned for production across the model range. Special versions, such as the Chiron Super Sport, pushed performance even further, demonstrating Bugatti’s commitment to speed and innovation. Production ended in 2024, marking the close of the W16 Chiron era and paving the way for Bugatti’s next generation of hypercars.
