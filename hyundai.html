Brief History on Hyundai:

Hyundai Motor Company was founded in 1967 in South Korea by Chung Ju-yung, as part of the Hyundai Group. The company initially focused on assembling vehicles under licence from international manufacturers before producing its first independently developed model, the Hyundai Pony, in 1975. Designed with help from international engineers, the Pony became South Korea’s first mass-produced car and helped Hyundai establish itself in the automotive industry. During the 1980s, Hyundai expanded internationally, entering the United States market in 1986 with the Excel, which became popular due to its affordable price. Throughout the 1990s and 2000s, Hyundai invested heavily in vehicle quality, technology, safety and design, gradually improving its reputation worldwide. The company also expanded its range to include SUVs, luxury vehicles through its Genesis brand, and environmentally friendly vehicles such as hybrids, electric cars and hydrogen fuel-cell vehicles. Today, Hyundai is one of the world's major automotive manufacturers, operating in markets across the globe and continuing to develop electric and smart mobility technologies.
