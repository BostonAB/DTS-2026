Lamborghini Diablo

The Lamborghini Diablo is an iconic Italian supercar produced by Lamborghini from 1990 to 2001, replacing the Countach as the company's flagship model. It was powered by a naturally aspirated 5.7-litre V12 engine in its early versions, producing around 485 horsepower. Later versions received larger and more powerful engines, with the Diablo GT producing around 575 horsepower. The Diablo was known for its dramatic wedge-shaped design, wide stance, scissor doors, and impressive performance, with early versions capable of reaching speeds of over 325 km/h.

Throughout its production, Lamborghini introduced several variants, including the VT, SV, SV-R, GT, and 6.0 SE. Some versions introduced all-wheel drive, improved aerodynamics, and more powerful engines, making the Diablo increasingly capable both on the road and track. The Diablo became an important part of Lamborghini's history, combining the extreme styling of the Countach with more modern technology and performance. It was eventually replaced by the Lamborghini Murciélago in 2001.
